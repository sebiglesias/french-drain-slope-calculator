Frequently Asked Questions

What is the minimum slope required for a gravity French drain?
A French drain requires a minimum downward slope of 1% (which equals 1/8 inch of drop per linear foot, or 1 inch of drop every 8 feet). For high-volume downspout tie-ins, a 2% slope (1/4 inch drop per foot) is ideal to prevent silt and debris from settling in the bottom of the pipe.
What should you do if your yard is completely flat with no natural slope?
If the surface ground is flat, you must dig the trench deeper as you travel away from the house. For a 50-foot trench starting at 18 inches deep, you will manually dig the discharge end down to 24 inches deep to artificially manufacture the necessary 6-inch gravity fall.
Should the holes in a perforated French drain pipe face UP or DOWN?
Always face the holes DOWN! Groundwater naturally rises from beneath the trench as the soil saturates. Perforations facing downward allow rising groundwater to enter the pipe at its lowest point and quickly drain away. If holes face up, water must fill the entire gravel trench before draining.
